Weekend readiness drop
Highest impact
Your Mon–Thu readiness averages 82.3–84.0. Friday drops to 78.2, Saturday to 77.9. That 4–6 point consistent drop is your weekend pattern showing up in biometric data — not just behaviour. Alcohol suppresses HRV and raises resting heart rate for 24–48hrs, which Oura reads directly as reduced readiness.
March stress cluster — the stall explained
Key finding
March had 9 stressful days — the highest of any month since October. This coincides exactly with your body fat stall beginning. Elevated cortisol from sustained stress promotes fat retention and can reduce motivation to train hard. The stall isn't just about calories — stress physiology is part of the picture.
Sleep quality is improving
Positive trend
Monthly average sleep score has risen from 79.3 in December to 84.5 in May — the best month in the dataset. This matters for body composition: poor sleep elevates ghrelin (hunger hormone) and reduces leptin (satiety), making caloric control harder. Better sleep is working in your favour right now.
Friday is your highest-risk day
Watch this
Friday readiness (78.2) is actually lower than Saturday (77.9 from previous night's sleep). This suggests the cumulative week — tennis Tuesday and Thursday, weights Mon/Wed/Fri — is leaving you depleted by the end of the week even before the weekend. Consider whether Friday lifting intensity needs more care given the fatigue context.
The practical lever
Action
To reach 18–19% body fat by July 17th your weekly recovery pattern needs to stop resetting. One concrete change: stop drinking by 9pm on Saturdays rather than cutting out alcohol. The timing matters — alcohol consumed after 10pm has the largest impact on sleep architecture and next-day readiness.
